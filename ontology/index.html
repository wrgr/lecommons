Shared vocabulary · Working draft 0.1.0

Learning engineering,
connected.

A vocabulary for describing the people, problems, methods and evidence involved in supporting learning.

For review. Grounded in IEEE ICICLE. Definitions and relationships are proposed; this is not an IEEE standard or an ICICLE-endorsed ontology.
VocabularyCompetenciesWorked exampleSourcesFormatted JSON

The foundation

Process and practice

Applying learning sciences through human-centered and engineering design, using iterative data-informed decisions to support learners and learning.

Paraphrase of ICICLE’s published definition.

Distinctions that matter

Desired ≠ observed. An intended result is not evidence of achievement.

Data ≠ evidence ≠ decisions. Observations need interpretation; choices need a rationale.

Iteration throughout. Activities may overlap and revisit earlier work.

Proposed next extension

Make competencies useful through evidence.

Competencies can connect the work to team development, role design and assessment. This extension is a proposal for review, not part of ICICLE’s published definition.

  1. RoleWho contributes?
  2. CompetencyWhat should they be able to do?
  3. TaskWhat work demonstrates it?
  4. EvidenceWhat performance was observed?

Attach context, expected proficiency, assessment criteria, support conditions and provenance. Keep required competency separate from demonstrated proficiency. A role title, course completion or successful model response alone does not establish competence.

Questions the ontology should answer

  1. Which challenge does an effort address, for which learners, and in which context?
  2. Which learning-science knowledge and design methods inform the effort?
  3. Which desired outcomes motivated a solution, and which outcomes were actually observed?
  4. How were observations captured and interpreted as evidence for a decision?
  5. What did an iteration change, why, and with whose participation?
  6. Which expertise contributed to the effort?

These are questions for checking the model’s coverage; they are distinct from people’s competencies.

An illustrative case

From a challenge to a revision

Invented workplace example. It illustrates the vocabulary and is not empirical evidence.

challenge
New employees have difficulty completing a troubleshooting task.
learner
New employees participating in onboarding.
context
Workplace onboarding with supervised access to equipment.
desired Outcome
Employees complete the task independently and correctly.
solution
A guided simulation followed by supervised practice.
instrumentation
Define task-success criteria and collect attempts and rubric ratings.
data
Attempt records and observer ratings.
observed Outcome
Some participants still omit a diagnostic check.
evidence
The recurring omission suggests a candidate weakness to investigate; it does not establish its cause.
decision
Revise the relevant practice activity and examine subsequent performance.
iteration
Introduce the revised activity while preserving comparable measurement procedures.

How to interpret this draft
  • All term definitions and relation labels below are proposed wording. Source references establish grounding, not endorsement.
  • Modules organize the vocabulary; they are not mutually exclusive categories or formal superclass axioms.
  • A relation describes a possible connection between instances of the named concepts. It is not mandatory for every instance.
  • Creation, implementation, investigation, and iteration can overlap. Their listing order does not prescribe a workflow.
  • Learning engineering has both process and practice aspects. Their distinction is a modeling choice, not a claim that ICICLE defines two separate entities.
  • Data, evidence, and decisions remain distinct. Observations do not automatically establish effectiveness or causation.
  • Desired outcomes and observed outcomes remain distinct. Intent does not establish achievement.
  • Learner and team-member terms describe participation roles. The same person may participate in several roles.
  • This draft specifies conceptual relationships, not formal OWL restrictions, cardinalities, or validation rules.

Open modeling questions
  • Should knowledge, claims, measures, instruments, and decision rationales become separately defined concepts?
  • How should an effort represent multiple iterations and changing context over time?
  • Which relations need qualifiers for time, uncertainty, evidence quality, or provenance?
  • Which extensions are needed for technology standards, accessibility, ethics, and organizational adoption?
  • Would the community prefer a controlled vocabulary first, followed by a formally constrained ontology?
